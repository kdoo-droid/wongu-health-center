On Your Feet All Shift: Foot, Knee & Back Pain Relief for Vegas Workers

By the Wongu Health Center team · October 9, 2026 · 5 min read

You know the feeling. You finally sit down in your car after a double, and your feet are throbbing before you even turn the key. Your lower back is tight, your knees ache, and tomorrow you'll do it all again. Servers carrying trays across the casino floor, housekeepers pushing carts and making beds, bartenders, cooks on the line, bellhops, and retail staff on the Strip keep this city running on their feet. If that's you, this one's for you.

It's Not Just You: Standing Takes a Real Toll

Your pain is real, and it has a name. A large review of 49 studies with more than 88,000 workers found that standing for more than about four hours a workday is linked to more low back pain and more pain in the legs and feet.[1] Hard floors, heavy trays, bending over carts, and rushing during a busy dinner service all add to it.

Hotel housekeepers have it especially hard. A study of hotel workers across five major U.S. hotel companies found housekeepers had the highest injury rate of any hotel job, including the highest rate of muscle and joint injuries.[2] If you clean rooms for a living, the ache in your back and shoulders at the end of the day isn't weakness. It's the work.

Where It Usually Hurts

  • Heels and arches. Sharp heel pain with your first steps in the morning is a classic sign of plantar fasciitis, very common in people who stand all day.
  • Knees. Hours on hard floors, plus squatting to stock or clean, can leave knees stiff and sore.
  • Lower back. Standing in one spot, leaning over a bar or prep table, or lifting linens and trays all load the lower back.
  • Calves, hips, and shoulders. Tight calves, achy hips, and sore shoulders from carrying and pushing often come along for the ride.

How Acupuncture May Help

Acupuncture is one of the most studied natural approaches for chronic pain. A major analysis that pooled data from nearly 21,000 patients across 39 trials found acupuncture helped with chronic back and neck pain, osteoarthritis (including knee pain), and other ongoing pain, and the benefit largely lasted at a one-year follow-up.[3] For heel pain from plantar fasciitis, a review of clinical studies found promising results, especially when acupuncture was added to usual care.[4]

Many of our patients come in tight and sore after a long week and tell us they leave feeling lighter and looser. Your practitioner may combine acupuncture with cupping for tight back and calf muscles, and will shape your treatment around what hurts and how your work week actually looks.

Simple Relief You Can Start Tonight

  • Invest in your shoes. Supportive, cushioned work shoes (and replacing them when the cushion wears out) can make a big difference. Supportive insoles help too.
  • Try compression socks if your legs feel heavy or swollen by the end of a shift.
  • Shift your weight. If you stand in one spot, rest one foot on a low ledge or rail and switch every few minutes.
  • Roll your feet. After work, roll each foot over a frozen water bottle or a tennis ball for a few minutes.
  • Stretch your calves against a wall before and after your shift. Tight calves pull on your heels.
  • Put your feet up. Ten minutes with your legs up the wall when you get home can feel amazing.
