Behind the Table: Neck, Shoulder & Wrist Pain Relief for Dealers

By the Wongu Health Center team · October 9, 2026 · 5 min read

Pitch, chip, sweep, repeat. Hour after hour, you're standing at the table, looking down at the layout, reaching across the felt, and keeping a smile on for the players. By the end of the shift, your neck is stiff, there's a knot between your shoulder blades that never quite goes away, and your wrists ache, sometimes with a little tingling in your fingers at night. If that sounds familiar, you're far from the only one in this town who feels it.

Why Dealing Is So Hard on Your Body

Dealing looks effortless when you do it well, but your body is working hard the whole time:

  • Looking down at the table for hours puts steady strain on the muscles at the back of your neck.
  • Reaching across the layout to pay and collect works your shoulders over and over, often with your arms held out in front of you.
  • Fast, repeated hand movements (pitching cards, cutting chips, shuffling, spinning) put a lot of mileage on your wrists, hands, and forearms.
  • Standing still on a hard floor adds tension through your lower back and legs.

The same pattern shows up in a lot of Vegas jobs: bartenders, cooks prepping on the line, cashiers, and anyone who uses their hands and arms all day. Repetitive strain builds slowly, which is why so many people push through it until it really starts to hurt.

How Acupuncture May Help

Neck and shoulder pain are among the conditions acupuncture has been studied for most. A large analysis pooling data from nearly 21,000 patients in 39 clinical trials found acupuncture helped with chronic neck pain and shoulder pain, and the benefit largely held up a year later.[1]

For wrist and hand symptoms like those of carpal tunnel syndrome (numbness, tingling, or aching in the thumb and first fingers, often worse at night), research is still growing. A 2023 review of clinical trials found acupuncture may help ease symptoms, and researchers are calling for larger studies.[2]

Many of our patients tell us their shoulders finally “let go” on the treatment table. Your practitioner may combine acupuncture with cupping for that stubborn upper-back knot, and will plan your care around your rotation and days off.

Easy Stretches for Your Break

These take a minute or two and can be done in the break room. Move gently, and stop if anything hurts.

  • Chin tucks. Sit or stand tall and gently slide your chin straight back, like making a double chin. Hold for a few seconds and repeat 5 to 10 times. This counters all that looking down.
  • Shoulder rolls. Roll your shoulders up, back, and down 10 times to loosen the tops of your shoulders.
  • Doorway chest stretch. Place your forearms on either side of a doorway and lean forward gently until you feel a stretch across your chest. Hold for 20 to 30 seconds.
  • Wrist stretches. With your arm straight out, gently pull your fingers back toward you with the other hand, then press the back of your hand to stretch the other way. Hold each for 15 to 20 seconds.
  • Shake it out. Let your arms hang and gently shake your hands for a few seconds to release tension.

At home, a warm shower or heating pad on your neck and shoulders can help you unwind after a shift.

Listen to Your Body

Please check in with your doctor if you have numbness or weakness that doesn't go away, if you start dropping things, if pain shoots down your arm, or if pain wakes you up most nights. These are worth a proper look, and acupuncture can be part of your care alongside your doctor's advice. You can also read about how we help with tension headaches, which often come along with a tight neck and shoulders.
