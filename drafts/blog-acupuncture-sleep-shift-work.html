Acupuncture for Sleep: A Guide for Las Vegas Shift Workers

By the Wongu Health Center team · October 9, 2026 · 5 min read

Las Vegas never sleeps, and the people who keep it running often don't get to either. Dealers, servers, cooks, security officers, nurses, and hotel staff work graveyard and rotating shifts so the rest of the city can enjoy itself. If you're one of them and you're running on empty, you're not alone, and it's not a personal failing. Your body is doing its best on a schedule it wasn't built for. Here's how acupuncture and a few simple habits may help you rest better.

Why Shift Work Makes Sleep So Hard

Your body runs on an internal clock, your circadian rhythm, which takes most of its cues from light. Daylight tells your brain it's time to be awake; darkness tells it to wind down. Night shifts ask your body to do the opposite, and rotating shifts keep changing the schedule before your body can settle in.

When this leads to ongoing trouble sleeping or feeling heavy and foggy at work, it's sometimes called shift work disorder. It's common: one study estimated it affects close to 1 in 5 night-shift nurses.[1]

How Acupuncture May Help

Acupuncture has been studied for sleep for many years, and the findings are encouraging. A 2021 review of 11 studies suggested acupuncture may help improve insomnia, and reviews of ear acupuncture have pointed the same way.[2] Researchers are now running larger trials, including studies focused on shift workers.[1]

Many of our patients tell us they feel calmer after a session and sleep more deeply in the days that follow. For people who'd like a gentle, drug-free option, it's well worth trying. Every treatment at our clinic uses sterile, single-use needles, and most people find the experience deeply relaxing. Some even fall asleep on the table.

Care That Fits Your Life

In Chinese medicine, sleep trouble isn't one-size-fits-all. Lying awake with a racing mind, waking at 3 a.m., restless dreams, or waking up still tired can each call for a different approach, and stress, digestion, and aches often play a part. Your practitioner will listen to how your schedule actually works, then put together a plan just for you. That may include acupuncture, an herbal tea or formula (after checking your medications), and small changes you can realistically fit into your week.

Simple Ways to Rest Better Between Shifts

You don't have to do all of these. Pick one or two that feel doable and build from there:

  • Make your bedroom a cave. Blackout curtains or a comfortable sleep mask help a lot, because the Vegas sun gets through ordinary blinds.
  • Keep it cool and quiet. Run the AC or a fan, and try earplugs or a white-noise app to soften daytime sounds.
  • Give yourself a wind-down routine. A shower, a light snack, or a few minutes of stretching can signal to your body that it's time to rest, even at 8 a.m.
  • Keep sleep times steady when you can, including on days off, if your schedule allows.
  • Ease off caffeine about six hours before bed, and try not to rely on alcohol to fall asleep, since it tends to make sleep lighter later on.
  • Protect your rest. Silence your phone and let the people you live with know your sleep hours.

Getting Home Safely

The drive home after a long night is when tiredness can catch up with you. If you're struggling to keep your eyes open, it's okay to take a short nap before you leave, or to get a ride home instead. Your safety matters more than getting home a little sooner.

You Deserve Support

If sleep has been a struggle for months, or you snore loudly, wake up gasping, or feel very low or anxious, please also check in with your doctor. Those can be signs of something like sleep apnea or depression that deserves care of its own, and acupuncture can work right alongside it. If you're ever having thoughts of harming yourself, you can call or text 988 any time to reach the Suicide & Crisis Lifeline.
