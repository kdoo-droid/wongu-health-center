Cupping Marks: What They Are and How Long They Last

By the Wongu Health Center team · October 9, 2026 · 4 min read

If you've seen an athlete with round purple circles across their back, you've seen cupping marks. They can look surprising the first time, and they're one of the most common things new patients ask us about. The short answer: they're a normal, temporary part of cupping, and most people find the treatment itself relaxing. Here's what the marks are, how long they last, and how to take care of yourself afterward.

What Causes the Marks?

Cupping uses gentle suction to lift the skin and the tissue just beneath it. That draws blood toward the surface, and a little of it collects under the skin. The round mark you see afterward is that blood, much like a bruise, except it comes from lifting instead of pressing.

The marks usually don't hurt. Most patients say the area feels warm or a little tender for a day or so, and many tell us their muscles feel looser and lighter after a session.

Why Are Some Marks Darker Than Others?

It's common for one cup to leave a light pink ring and the cup next to it a deeper purple one. Research has found that stronger suction and longer cup time tend to leave darker marks.[1] Color can also depend on:

  • How easily your skin bruises in general
  • Whether the cups stayed in place or were glided across the skin
  • Your skin tone
  • Medications that affect bleeding, such as blood thinners

In traditional Chinese medicine, practitioners also notice mark color as one small part of understanding how your body is doing. A darker mark isn't a sign that something is wrong with you.

How Long Do Cupping Marks Last?

Most marks fade within a few days to about two weeks. Lighter marks are often gone in two or three days. Darker ones usually take one to two weeks, softening from purple to brown to yellow as they fade.

Have a wedding, a pool day, or photos coming up? Just let your practitioner know. We can use lighter suction, shorter cup times, or keep the cups to areas your clothes will cover. Your comfort comes first.

Taking Care of Yourself After Cupping

  • Keep the area covered and warm for the rest of the day, especially in air-conditioning.
  • Save hot showers, saunas, and hot tubs for the next day.
  • Drink some water and give yourself permission to rest if you feel tired.
  • Let the marks fade before cupping the same area again.
  • Mention your cupping to any doctor, nurse, or massage therapist who might see the marks, so they know what they are.[2]

We're Here if You Have Questions

Cupping is well tolerated by most people. Like any treatment, it occasionally has side effects, such as blisters, skin irritation, or a flare of a skin condition like eczema.[2] Please reach out to us if you notice:

  • Blisters or broken skin
  • Redness, warmth, or swelling that's increasing instead of settling
  • Marks that haven't started fading after a week or so
  • Pain that feels sharp rather than mildly tender

Before your first session, let us know if you take blood thinners, bruise easily, are pregnant, or have a skin condition. That way your practitioner can tailor the treatment to you.

How Cupping Can Help

People have used cupping for centuries, and many of our patients look forward to it for relief from muscle tension, tight shoulders, and back stiffness. A review of 135 clinical trials found that cupping may help with pain conditions such as neck and back pain.[3] Researchers are still running larger, high-quality studies to learn more.[2] We often combine cupping with acupuncture, and your practitioner will help you decide what fits your goals. Learn more on our cupping therapy page.
