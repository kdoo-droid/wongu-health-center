Cupping Marks: What They Are and How Long They Last

By the Wongu Health Center team · October 9, 2026 · 4 min read

If you have seen an athlete with perfect purple circles across their back, you have seen cupping marks. They look dramatic, and they are the number one thing new patients ask us about before trying cupping. Here is what the marks are, how long they usually last, and what is and isn't normal.

What Causes the Marks?

Cupping uses suction to lift the skin and the tissue just beneath it into a cup. That pull draws blood toward the surface, and some of the tiny capillaries under the skin leak a little blood into the surrounding tissue. What you see afterward is that blood under the skin, much like a bruise, except it's caused by suction instead of impact.

The marks usually aren't painful. Most patients describe the area as feeling a little warm or tender to the touch for a day or so, and many say the muscles underneath feel looser.

Why Are Some Marks Darker Than Others?

Patients are often surprised when one cup leaves a faint pink ring and the cup next to it leaves a deep purple one. Research on cupping has found that stronger suction and longer cup time tend to produce darker marks.[1] Other things that can affect color include:

  • How easily your skin bruises in general
  • Whether the cups were left still or moved across the skin (“sliding” cupping)
  • Your skin tone, which can make marks look lighter or darker
  • Medications that affect bleeding, such as blood thinners

In traditional Chinese medicine, practitioners also pay attention to mark color as part of their assessment. That's a traditional interpretation, not something modern research has confirmed, so we don't treat a dark mark as a diagnosis on its own.

How Long Do Cupping Marks Last?

Most cupping marks fade within a few days to about two weeks. Light marks may be gone in two or three days; darker ones usually take one to two weeks, changing from purple to brownish to yellow as they fade, the same way a bruise does.

If you have an event coming up (a wedding, a pool party, a photo shoot), tell your practitioner. We can use lighter suction, shorter cup times, or keep cups to areas your clothes will cover.

Caring for Your Skin After Cupping

  • Keep the area covered and warm for the rest of the day, especially in air-conditioning.
  • Skip very hot showers, saunas, and hot tubs for the rest of the day.
  • Drink water and take it easy if you feel tired.
  • Wait for the marks to fade before cupping the same area again.
  • Mention the marks to any doctor, nurse, or massage therapist who might see them, so they aren't mistaken for an injury. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health (NCCIH) gives this same advice.[2]

When to Call Us

Cupping is generally well tolerated, but it isn't risk-free. NCCIH notes that cupping can occasionally cause burns, blisters, infection, scarring, or lasting skin discoloration, and can make skin conditions such as eczema or psoriasis worse.[2] Contact the clinic, or your doctor, if you notice:

  • Blisters, open skin, or signs of infection (increasing redness, warmth, swelling, or pus)
  • Marks that are getting worse instead of fading after a few days
  • Marks that haven't faded at all after three weeks
  • Pain that is sharp or getting worse rather than mild tenderness

Before your first session, tell your practitioner if you take blood thinners, bruise easily, are pregnant, or have a skin condition, so they can adjust the treatment or recommend something else.

Does Cupping Work?

We want to be straightforward about this. A large review of 135 randomized trials found that cupping may help with some pain conditions, such as neck and back pain, but most of those studies were small or had design problems.[3] NCCIH describes the evidence that cupping may reduce pain as promising but not very strong.[2] Many of our patients find it helps with muscle tension and stiffness, and we often combine it with acupuncture. Your practitioner can talk with you about whether it makes sense for you. You can learn more on our cupping therapy page.
