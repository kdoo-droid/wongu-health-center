Conditions We Treat

What patients come to Wongu Health Center for, and an honest picture of where acupuncture and Chinese medicine can help. We always work alongside your medical care, not in place of it.

What the research says

Acupuncture has been studied in thousands of clinical trials. The evidence is strong for some conditions and still developing for others. Here is a summary, drawn from the National Institutes of Health's research summary, clinical practice guidelines, and Cochrane reviews, which pool the best available trials on a question.1

Strongest evidence

Supported by large reviews and recommended in clinical practice guidelines:

  • Chronic low back pain. The American College of Physicians lists acupuncture among the first-line, non-drug treatments for chronic low back pain.2
  • Chronic pain in general. An analysis of data from nearly 21,000 patients in 39 trials found acupuncture worked better than both sham acupuncture and usual care for chronic back and neck pain, osteoarthritis, chronic headache, and shoulder pain, and most of the benefit was still there a year later.3 England's national health guidance (NICE) recommends considering a course of acupuncture for chronic primary pain.4
  • Knee and hip osteoarthritis. Reviews found acupuncture eased osteoarthritis pain more than sham treatment, with an effect comparable to anti-inflammatory drugs, and the American College of Rheumatology conditionally recommends it.1,5
  • Migraine prevention. A Cochrane review of 22 trials (almost 5,000 people) found moderate-quality evidence that acupuncture reduces how often migraines happen, working at least as well as common preventive medicines with fewer side effects.6
  • Tension-type headache. In a Cochrane review of 12 trials, 48 in 100 people having acupuncture saw their headache days cut at least in half, compared with 17 in 100 on usual care alone.7
  • Nausea after surgery. Stimulating the PC6 acupuncture point on the wrist reduced nausea and vomiting after surgery compared with sham treatment.8
  • Joint pain from breast cancer medication. Oncology guidelines from ASCO and the Society for Integrative Oncology recommend offering acupuncture for joint pain caused by aromatase inhibitors.9

Promising, but the evidence is mixed

Studies suggest a possible benefit, but the trials are smaller, lower quality, or the advantage over sham treatment is unclear:

  • Insomnia. A 2021 review of 11 studies suggested acupuncture may help, but the studies were small.10
  • Depression. A 2018 review of 64 studies suggested acupuncture may moderately reduce depression, but most of the studies were low quality.1
  • Anxiety. Many studies report benefits, but their quality varies widely, so firm conclusions aren't possible yet.11
  • Menopausal hot flashes. Acupuncture reduced hot flashes compared with no acupuncture, but not compared with sham acupuncture.1
  • Nausea from chemotherapy. Experts generally agree acupuncture helps, but that view rests mostly on older research.1
  • Cupping. Cupping may help with some kinds of pain, but most studies are low quality.12

Research on Chinese herbal medicine varies from herb to herb, so your practitioner will talk with you about the specific formula and check it against any medications you take.

Other reasons patients visit us

Beyond the in-depth guides above, patients come to us for the concerns below. Where the research is mixed, we describe acupuncture and herbal medicine as supportive care: it belongs alongside the care you get from your physician or other providers. Your practitioner will talk with you honestly about what to expect.

Neck, shoulder, and joint pain

Neck and shoulder tension, knee and joint pain, and osteoarthritis. After back pain, this is where the research on acupuncture is strongest (see above). Acupuncture and cupping are often combined for muscle tension.

Stress, sleep, and emotional wellness

Research on acupuncture for sleep, anxiety, and depression is promising but still developing. We offer it as supportive care for stress, sleep, and anxious feelings, alongside appropriate medical or mental-health care, including for veterans. Read 5 ways acupuncture may help with stress.

Women's health

Supportive care for menstrual concerns, fertility, pregnancy, and menopause. Dr. Eunhee Yu, our clinic director, has years of experience in fertility and women's health, and Dr. Yu and Dr. Sekine offer structured multi-visit packages for pregnancy and birth. Because the research here is mixed, we work alongside your OB-GYN or fertility specialist rather than in place of them. Please tell your practitioner if you are pregnant or trying to conceive.

Digestion and long-term wellness

Ongoing digestive concerns, low energy, and chronic conditions that benefit from steady support, always alongside your doctor's care. Chinese herbal formulas are often part of care here.

Recovery and support during medical treatment

Supportive care for people working toward mobility and comfort goals after an illness or injury, or for nausea, fatigue, sleep, and stress during medical treatment, including joint pain from breast cancer medication (see above). This care is always coordinated with your medical team. It does not replace neurological care, rehabilitation, or cancer treatment.

What we don't treat

Acupuncture is not emergency care, and it is not a substitute for diagnosis by a physician. If you have chest pain, signs of a stroke, a sudden severe headache, a high fever, or any other emergency, call 911. If you have a new, worsening, or unexplained symptom, see your doctor first. We are happy to see you once serious causes have been ruled out.
