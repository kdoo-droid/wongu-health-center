Chinese Herbs and Your Medications: What to Tell Your Practitioner

By the Wongu Health Center team · October 9, 2026 · 5 min read

Many of our patients come to Chinese herbal medicine while also taking medicine from their doctor for blood pressure, diabetes, or something else. That's completely normal, and herbs and modern medicine can often work side by side. Because herbs have real effects on the body, the key to doing this safely is simple: make sure your practitioner knows everything you take. Here's why that matters, and how we take care of it together.

Why Your Full Medication List Matters

Herbs and medicines can sometimes affect each other in two ways:

  • An herb can change how your body processes a medicine, so the medicine ends up a little stronger or weaker than intended.
  • An herb and a medicine can do similar things. For example, an herb that supports circulation, taken with a blood thinner, can add up to more effect than either one alone.

Researchers are still learning about many of these combinations.[1] That's why our practitioners take a thoughtful, personalized approach, choosing herbs that fit both your health goals and the medicines you already rely on.

Medicines We'll Ask About

Blood thinners (warfarin, apixaban, clopidogrel, daily aspirin)

Some well-known herbs, including Dang Gui (Angelica root) and Dan Shen (Salvia root), may increase the effect of warfarin.[2] Ginkgo is another herb to be careful with alongside blood thinners.[1] If you take a blood thinner, your practitioner will simply build your formula around it, and can coordinate with your doctor if that would help.

Blood pressure and heart medicines

Gan Cao (licorice root) is used in small amounts in many classic formulas to help the other herbs work in harmony. In larger amounts over time, licorice can raise blood pressure and lower potassium,[3] so we'll adjust it if you take blood pressure medicine, water pills, or digoxin.

Diabetes medicines

Some herbs, such as ginseng, may gently affect blood sugar. If you have diabetes, keep checking your levels as usual when you start a formula, and let us know how you're doing so we can fine-tune things together.

Antidepressants, transplant and HIV medicines, and birth control

These medicines are sensitive to many supplements. St. John's wort, a common Western herb, is the best-known example and can make some medicines, including birth control, less effective.[1] If you take any of these, it helps to share every supplement you use, not just the herbs we recommend.

What to Bring to Your Appointment

The easiest thing is to bring a list, or simply bring your bottles in a bag. Please include:

  • Your prescription medicines and doses
  • Over-the-counter medicines you use often (pain relievers, allergy pills, antacids)
  • Vitamins, supplements, protein powders, and wellness teas
  • Herbs or herbal products from anywhere else
  • Any upcoming surgery or dental work, since some herbs are paused beforehand
  • Whether you're pregnant, breastfeeding, or trying to conceive

It's also a good idea to let your doctor and pharmacist know about your herbal formula. When everyone caring for you has the full picture, you get the best of both.

How We Look After You

At Wongu Health Center, your formula is chosen for you after we review your health history, medications, symptoms, tongue, and pulse. Our senior interns work under the supervision of licensed Oriental Medicine Doctors. If a particular herb isn't the right fit with your medicines, there's almost always another good option, and acupuncture is always available too.

Tell your practitioner about all medications and supplements you take. Herbal formulas may interact with medications and may not be appropriate during pregnancy or for certain medical conditions.

Please keep taking your prescriptions as your doctor directed. Herbs are meant to work alongside your care, not replace it. If anything feels off after starting a formula, such as unusual bruising, dizziness, swelling, or a rash, pause the herbs and call us and your doctor so we can help. You can learn more on our Chinese herbal medicine page.

Have Questions About Herbs and Your Medications?

Bring your medication list to your visit and we'll go through it with you.
