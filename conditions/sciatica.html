Acupuncture for Sciatica

Sciatica is burning, shooting, or aching pain that runs from the lower back or buttock down the leg. Here is how acupuncture may help, what recent research found, and when to see a doctor first.

Sciatica isn't a diagnosis on its own. It describes pain along the sciatic nerve, which runs from the lower spine through the buttock and down the back of each leg. It is most often caused by a herniated or bulging disc pressing on a nerve root. Narrowing of the spinal canal (stenosis) or a tight piriformis muscle in the buttock can cause it too.

Common signs of sciatica

  • Pain that travels from the low back or buttock down the back of one leg, sometimes to the foot
  • Burning, electric, or shooting pain rather than a dull ache
  • Tingling or pins-and-needles in the leg or foot
  • Pain that is worse with sitting, coughing, or sneezing

Get medical care right away if you have:

  • loss of bladder or bowel control, or numbness in the groin, buttocks, or inner thighs ("saddle" area),
  • weakness in the leg or foot that is getting worse, or a foot that drags or slaps when you walk,
  • sciatica in both legs at once, or
  • sciatica after a significant fall or accident.

These can signal serious nerve compression that needs urgent medical evaluation. Acupuncture is not appropriate until a physician has assessed you.

What the research says

Until recently, evidence for acupuncture in sciatica was thin. In 2024, JAMA Internal Medicine published a randomized trial of 216 adults with chronic sciatica from a herniated disc. One group received 10 acupuncture sessions over four weeks, and the other received sham (placebo) acupuncture. The acupuncture group had noticeably less leg pain and better function. The difference showed up by week two and lasted through the one-year follow-up.1

That trial was carried out at hospitals in China, and one trial isn't the final word. Its results are encouraging, though, and they fit the broader evidence that acupuncture helps chronic back pain.2 For many people with sciatica, acupuncture is a reasonable, low-risk option alongside medical care and physical therapy.

What treatment at Wongu involves

Assessment. Your practitioner will ask where the pain travels, what triggers it, how long you've had it, and whether you've had imaging or a diagnosis. Bring any MRI reports or doctor's notes you have. They help us plan your care and spot anything that should go back to your physician.

Treatment. Needles are usually placed in the lower back and buttock and along the path of the pain in the leg, plus other points chosen from your overall assessment. Treatment is adjusted to how irritable your nerve pain is. The first visit is often gentler.

Additional care. Depending on your case, your practitioner may add cupping for tight surrounding muscles, heat, or a herbal formula. Staying gently active and following your physical therapist's exercises usually helps recovery.

How many sessions?

The 2024 trial used 10 sessions over four weeks, which is more frequent than many people expect. Your practitioner will suggest a schedule based on how severe and how long-standing your symptoms are, and will reassess with you after the first few weeks. If you aren't seeing meaningful improvement, or your symptoms change, we'll recommend you follow up with your physician.

Intern or licensed OMD?

Both options are available for sciatica. For complex cases, such as recent surgery, severe disc disease, or several other conditions, you may prefer to start with one of our licensed OMDs. A supervised intern visit gives you a longer, lower-cost appointment, and a licensed OMD reviews every treatment.
