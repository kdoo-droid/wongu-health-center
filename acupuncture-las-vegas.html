What acupuncture at Wongu looks like

Acupuncture uses sterile, single-use needles about as thin as a hair, placed at specific points on the body. Most patients feel a brief pinch or a dull, spreading sensation. Then they rest for 20–30 minutes, and many fall asleep.

Every visit starts with a conversation. Your practitioner asks about your symptoms, health history, sleep, digestion, and stress, and checks your tongue and pulse as in traditional Chinese medicine. The points they choose follow from that assessment, so two people with "back pain" rarely get the same treatment.

Acupuncture here is offered as part of your overall care, not a replacement for it. We work alongside your physician, physical therapist, or other providers, and we will tell you when something should be checked by a medical doctor first.

What patients see us for

Most people come to us for pain that has lasted weeks or years, and research on acupuncture is strongest there. A large analysis of individual patient data from 39 randomized trials (about 20,000 patients) found acupuncture worked better than both sham acupuncture and usual care for chronic back and neck pain, osteoarthritis, and chronic headache. The benefit largely persisted a year later.1

Patients also seek acupuncture for neck and shoulder tension, joint pain and arthritis, and stress and sleep. Other reasons include digestive concerns, women's health, and comfort during medical treatment. For these, we describe acupuncture as supportive care. The evidence is more mixed, and it should sit alongside your medical or mental-health care. See all conditions for details.

What happens at your first visit

  1. Check-in. Arrive 10–15 minutes early. Parking at 8630 S Eastern Ave is free.
  2. Consultation. Your practitioner reviews your health history, medications, and goals, then does a tongue and pulse assessment.
  3. Treatment. Needles are placed, often on the arms, legs, back, or abdomen, and you rest for 20–30 minutes. Cupping or moxibustion (gentle heat from the herb mugwort) may be added if it suits you.
  4. Plan. You leave knowing what your practitioner suggests next: how often to come back, what to watch for, and whether herbs could help.

Our first-visit guide covers what to wear, what to eat beforehand, and how you may feel afterward.

How many sessions will I need?

It depends on what you are treating and for how long it has been going on. Recent, uncomplicated pain may change within a few visits. Long-standing conditions usually need a series, often weekly for several weeks, then spaced out as things improve. Your practitioner should give you a realistic plan and check your progress with you. If you are not seeing meaningful change after a fair trial, we will say so.

Is acupuncture safe?

In trained hands, with sterile single-use needles, acupuncture is generally considered safe. Side effects are usually minor: brief soreness, a small bruise, or feeling tired or lightheaded after treatment.2 Please tell your practitioner if you:

  • are pregnant or may be pregnant,
  • take blood-thinning medication or have a bleeding disorder,
  • have a pacemaker or other implanted device, or
  • have any condition you are being treated for.

Acupuncture is not emergency care

For chest pain, sudden weakness or numbness, the worst headache of your life, loss of bladder or bowel control, or any other emergency, call 911 or go to the nearest emergency room.
